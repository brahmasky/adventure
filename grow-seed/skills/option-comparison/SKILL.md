---
name: option-comparison
description: "comparing two or three options against stated criteria and recommending one"
example: "should I renew the lease or buy the car outright?"
origin: exemplar
anchors:
  - "a recommendation names the criterion that decided it"
---

1. Restate the decision in one line, and list the options exactly as given. If there are more than three, ask which three matter before going on.

2. Collect the criteria. Use the ones stated in the request first. Add at most two that are standard for this kind of decision, and mark them as added, so they can be struck out.

3. Ask for anything a criterion needs and the request does not give: a budget, a time horizon, a hard constraint. Ask once, for all of it. If there is no answer, state the assumption and continue.

4. For each option and criterion, write one short factual cell: a number with its unit, or a plain statement. Where a fact is unknown, write "unknown" and say what would settle it. Do not fill a gap with a guess.

5. Mark each criterion as a requirement (an option that fails it is out) or a preference (it is weighed). Remove any option that fails a requirement, and say which one it failed.

6. Compare the remaining options on preferences only. Say which option is better on each, and by how much when a number exists.

7. Recommend one option. Name the single criterion that decided it, and the condition under which the other option would be the better choice.

8. Present it as: the recommendation and its deciding criterion in two sentences; then the table; then the assumptions; then the unknowns that would change the answer.

9. Before sending, check: every cell has a source or is marked as an assumption; no criterion appears twice under two names; the recommendation follows from the table and nothing outside it.
